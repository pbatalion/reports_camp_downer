What This Number Tells You

Camp Downer's tiered pricing model does more than cover its costs. Families paid an average of $649 per camper week after scholarships, against $516 actually spent per camper week so far this year. Measured against the full annual budget rather than spending to date, the cost is $586 per camper week, still comfortably below tuition. Tuition alone keeps the camp sustainable, with no fundraising required to break even.

The honest caveat: $516 is low partly because $77,900 of the annual expense budget has not yet been spent, and roughly $29,350 of that is still expected to land before December 31. The $586 budget-based figure is the fairer number for pricing decisions, and the one reported in July. Either way the conclusion holds, and the gap is wider than planned.

How "Cost Per Camper Week" Is Calculated

A camper week is one camper attending for one week. Camp Downer delivered 1,113 camper weeks in Summer 2026, from 968 session registrations, counting the two-week Session 3 (145 campers) as two weeks each: 823 one-week registrations + (145 × 2) = 1,113.

Cost per camper week = total operating cost ÷ total camper weeks
Budget basis: $652,440 ÷ 1,113 ≈ $586
Actual spending to date: $574,540 ÷ 1,113 ≈ $516

This spreads every operating cost (staff, food, programs, facilities, admin and insurance) evenly across all the weeks of camp delivered. It is the all-in cost to serve one camper for one week, and it deliberately includes year-round costs, not just what is spent between June and August: the director's role is full-time year-round, and the facility has to be maintained, insured and heated in the off-season whether or not campers are on site. Tuition has to cover all twelve months, which is why the comparison against tuition is the right one.

Average net tuition per camper week = net tuition revenue ÷ total camper weeks
$722,032 ÷ 1,113 ≈ $649

Net tuition is the $749,392 enrolled less $27,360 in scholarships, blended across the three pricing tiers.

Verified against final registrations. The 1,113 figure is confirmed by the post-season Registration Summary run on September 23, 2026: 968 registered campers across seven sessions, with Session 3 (145 campers) counted as two weeks. No estimate is involved.

Final Summer 2026 enrollment finished at $749,392, up $1,207 from the $748,185 reported in July and $10,367 above the $739,025 projected back in May. Scholarships came to 3.7% of gross enrollment.

Behind the $27,360: per the Registrar's report, 49 families applied on behalf of 65 campers. Nine families were found ineligible on the income documentation provided and seven applied after the deadline and went to a waitlist. 31 families were awarded scholarships covering 45 campers, 31 of them full awards and 14 half awards, with three of those campers later cancelling. Separately, $3,250 of restricted scholarship donations came in, including $2,750 to the Joyce Dion Memorial Scholarship, against $27,360 awarded. The gap is funded from general operations.

Enrollment and Capacity

Summer 2026 ran at 98.5% of capacity: 968 campers registered against 983 available places, leaving just 15 spaces unfilled across the entire season. Three sessions sold out completely.

What stands out: Session 1 holds 9 of the 15 unfilled places, at 94% against a season that otherwise ran at 99% or better. Sessions 3 and 6 filled completely. The Directors' Annual Report does not give a reason for Session 1's slack, so it is worth asking the registrar rather than assuming one.

By roster, boys+ places ran at 97% (435 of 449) and girls+ at essentially 100% (533 of 534, a single open space all summer). Girls+ made up 55% of campers. If the camp wants to grow enrollment, the capacity constraint is on the girls+ side, not demand.

Demand was in fact stronger than these figures suggest. Per the Registrar's report, 997 individual campers registered across 805 households, and 109 later cancelled (68 before the May 1 deposit refund deadline and 41 after). Almost all of those places were refilled from the waitlist, which kept moving right up until sessions began. Half the cancellations were for Sessions 5 or 7, and 62% were girls+ campers. Of the 22 families who gave a reason, the most common were scheduling conflicts and a child being nervous about attending.

The practical implication: at 98.5% full, revenue growth cannot come from filling seats. There are only 15 of them, worth roughly $10,000 at current rates. Any material increase in tuition revenue has to come from pricing or from adding capacity.

The Bottom Line

Summer 2026 was a financially strong season. Enrolled revenue of $749,392 against operating costs that will finish well under the $652,440 budget leaves the camp with a projected surplus around $125,000, on a budget that planned for a $35,738 deficit. Average net tuition of $649 per camper week covers the $586 budgeted cost with room to spare, so the pricing model is sound and the tier mix is holding at roughly even thirds.

The question this puts to the board is not whether the model works, but what to do with the result: fund deferred capital work, widen scholarship access, or hold rates for 2027.
