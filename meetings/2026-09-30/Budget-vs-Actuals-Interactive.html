Understanding This Report: The season is complete. Unlike the July report, which was a mid-year snapshot with most spending still ahead, this compares a finished summer against the full-year plan. Expenses landed 12% below budget and net revenue reached $151,252 against a budgeted deficit of $35,738. Read the revenue variance with care: the budget is not built around the pricing tiers, so registrations sit above the budgeted line as a matter of course.
About the Registration Variance: Registrations always land above budget because the budget is built on a single rate and does not model the three pricing tiers. Families paying the Tier A and Tier B rates lift actual revenue above the budgeted figure every year, so the $159,683 variance reflects how the budget is constructed rather than a change in enrollment. Summer 2026 enrolled $749,392, in line with the final figure reported in July.
What's Left to Spend: $74,302 of the expense budget is unspent, but the Directors' Annual Report accounts for most of it line by line. Roughly $25,842 is genuinely still to come, the largest piece being Staff Salaries. That line is not seasonal: the director is full-time year-round, so it keeps accruing monthly after camp closes, and the director expects it to finish about $5,000 under budget rather than the $22,836 under it shows today. Administration will spend its remaining $3,760 on ACA and ACH memberships in December, and Electric, Telephone, Credit Card Processing and the Director line are all expected to meet budget. Another $33,400 will never be spent, but for specific reasons rather than as deferred work: see the note below. On that basis the year should close near $125,000, lower if year-end accounting and insurance also land.
The books are now current. The Directors' Annual Report had flagged that receipt entry into QuickBooks fell behind over the summer, leaving a backlog when the administrative assistant changed. The accountant has since confirmed QuickBooks is up to date, and these figures are the reconciled September 25 exports. Clearing the backlog added $3,598 of expenses and $500 of scholarship donations, moving net revenue from $154,350 to $151,252. Two thirds of the added expense was a single electric bill.
Two lines crossed over budget in the reconciliation. Electric went from 61% of budget to 104% after $3,049 posted, so a line that looked like a saving is now an overspend with a quarter of the year still to run. Payroll Fees also crossed to 106%, exactly as the Directors' report predicted. Both should be raised for 2027. Going the other way, Staff (non-salary) fell from 123% to 107% and Camper Store from 93% to 92%, which brings both into line with the figures the Directors' report quoted as of September 18.
Two underspends are not underspends. Per the Directors' Annual Report, Off-Season Projects ($12,575 "unspent") and Other Equipment ($4,004 "unspent") were both spent. CompuCount classified the maintenance shed renovation and the water system update as 1505 Leasehold Improvements, and the new washer and dryer as 1500 Equipment, so that money sits on the balance sheet as a fixed asset instead of appearing as an expense here. Dining Service is a third case: the $13,824 under budget comes from $14,289 of credits carried in from 2024 and 2025, not from spending less. The Fresh Picks contract itself was $129,465, slightly above the $129,000 budgeted.

Questions for the Board

  • The off-season budget was spent, just not shown here. The maintenance shed renovation and water system update were capitalised to Leasehold Improvements. Separately, the Directors' report lists further work awaiting estimates (rewiring cabins, hardwiring exhaust fans, steel gas lines, draining well #3) plus the Director's Cabin roof, driveway washout and Rec Hall floor.
  • One overage is a one-off, the other is a trend. The truck's $3,431 reflects specific repairs, with the leaf-spring job already scheduled for May 2027. Healthcare is different: the Directors' report says these costs "continue to increase" and asks for the budget to be raised for 2027, so it should not be treated as a one-off.
  • Where the surplus goes. The surplus accumulates in the operating account by default. Whether any of it should move into the investment portfolio is a November decision.
